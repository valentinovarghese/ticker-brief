Ticker Brief

Microsoft rose 16.6% and added about $490B in market value — the largest one-day gain any stock has ever made.

Thu 30 Jul 2026QQQ +3.5%written 14:15 ET · market open12 tickers · 5 with news

The 30-second version

  1. Microsoft's cloud growth sped up instead of slowing. The stock set an all-time record for value created in one session.
  2. Meta spent almost everything it earned and fell about 9%. Same spending story, opposite verdict — the reason is the most useful thing on this page.
  3. Apple and Amazon both report after the close tonight. Nothing is settled until then.
  4. Timing note. This edition was written at 2:15pm in New York, with the market open. Prices below are live intraday, not the previous close plus premarket. They can still move before the 4pm bell.

Market-wide

The Fed held rates at 3.50–3.75% for a fifth straight meeting, with three officials dissenting in favour of a rise
How it reaches these stocksThree regional presidents — Hammack, Kashkari and Logan — all wanted a quarter-point hike. That is the first three-way dissent in one direction since 2016. Rates staying high hurts fast-growing tech most. Their value sits in profits expected years out, and distant profits are worth less when money costs more.
Core PCE inflation has climbed from 3.0% in December to 3.4% in May
How it reaches these stocksCore PCE is the measure the Fed actually steers by. Inflation drifting up rather than down removes the room to cut. That is normally a direct headwind to anything priced on far-off growth.
The gauge contradicts all of it
The gaugeQQQ is up about 3.5%, on track for its best day since May 2025. Earnings are overriding macro today. But ten stocks account for 88% of that index move, from about a third of its weight. The rally is narrow, so it rests on a handful of results rather than broad demand — and the macro risk is still sitting there unpriced.

▲ MSFTMajor · bullish+16.6% · best day since 2008

Azure grew faster than last quarter, and Microsoft raised next year's building budget by about a third.

Revenue   $90.0B vs $87.6B expected  (+18% y/y)
EPS       $4.74 ex one-off, vs $4.24 expected

Contracted future revenue $678B, +84% y/y
FY27 capex $255–260B vs ~$190B this year

Why it mattersThe $678B of signed but undelivered work is the number that changes the argument. It means the spending is backed by orders already placed, not by hope. That turns capital expenditure from a cost investors fear into capacity they can count on. It also extends how many years of fast growth they are willing to assume — which lifts what they will pay per dollar of earnings, and moves the valuation more than the earnings beat itself.

Already priced in?Yes. A 16.6% move and $490B added is a full repricing. Largely priced in, on live intraday prices.

Why it mattersRaising the floor of a spending range is a commitment. Raising the ceiling is only an option. Meta removed its own escape route. Free cash flow — the cash left after paying for buildings and equipment — is now close to nothing, so the buffer that funded buybacks and absorbed weak quarters has gone. Margins falling 12 points in a year shows the spend hitting profits now, while any payoff sits years away.

The asymmetry worth understanding

Microsoft and Meta both raised spending sharply on the same night. One added $490B of value, the other lost about 9%. The difference is not the amount — it is what stands behind it. Microsoft can point to $678B of signed contracts. Meta cannot point to any. Investors call the first investment and the second cost. Ask that question of every capital-spending headline you read.

Why it mattersThis distinction is the whole point. A sector move can reverse as fast as it arrived, because nothing changed at the company itself. Intel's own quarter was genuinely strong a week ago, but the foundry business still sells only about $293M to outside customers. Until that number grows, Intel's value rests on the same industry-wide demand that lifted it today — and that can be taken back just as quickly.

Read-through

Where Microsoft's spending lands

A cloud company raising its building budget is not an isolated event — that money becomes revenue for the companies it buys from. Microsoft guiding to $255–260B is a direct positive for the chip suppliers (NVDA, MRVL, INTC) and for the smaller cloud builders chasing the same demand (NBIS). Together with Lam Research's results and Samsung's shortage warning, that is most of why chips rallied today on news that was technically about a software company.

Nothing material

TSLA · GOOGL · PLTR · NBIS · MRVL · NVDA — no company-specific events in the past 24 hours.

Two clarifications

MRVL and NVDA both rose with the chip sector on no company news of their own — sector beta, not events. And NBIS is genuinely quiet: its $1B compute contract with Reflection AI was signed on 14 July, so it is not a story for today, whatever the stock does.
