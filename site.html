Ticker Brief

Microsoft added $100B of cloud revenue and a $65B spending increase on the same night — and rose 15% for it.

Thu 30 Jul 2026QQQ +2.5–3%SOXX +8%12 tickers · 7 with news

The 30-second version

  1. Two big earnings reports pulled in opposite directions. Microsoft's cloud growth sped up and the stock had its best day since 2008. Meta's profit missed and it fell 9%.
  2. Both raised spending sharply. The market rewarded one and punished the other — the reason why is the most useful thing on this page.
  3. The Fed held rates but three officials voted to raise them. Tech ignored it completely and traded on earnings instead.

Market-wide

The Fed held rates steady, with three officials dissenting in favour of a hike
How it reaches these stocksFast-growing tech is priced on profits expected years from now, and those distant profits are worth less when interest rates stay high. Three votes to hike is the hawkish tail nobody had priced — it means the next move might be up rather than down. That matters more to Nvidia or Palantir than to a mature, cash-rich business.
Q2 GDP came in at 1.5%, below expectations, while annual PCE inflation held at 4%
How it reaches these stocksSlow growth with sticky inflation is the combination that removes the Fed's room to cut. Treasury yields rose sharply on the release. Normally that is a direct headwind to growth valuations.
The market ignored all of it
The gaugeQQQ rose roughly 2.5–3% and the semiconductor index gained 8%. When rate news this hawkish gets overridden inside a session, it means positioning is being driven by AI earnings, not by macro. Worth noting: that also means macro risk is still sitting there unpriced.

The asymmetry worth understanding

Microsoft and Meta both raised spending sharply on the same night. One rose 15%, the other fell 9%. The difference is not the amount — it is what the spending buys. Microsoft's turns into cloud capacity it has already signed customers for, so the spend arrives attached to contracted revenue. Meta's turns into better ad targeting and AI research, with no signed revenue behind it. Investors treat the first as investment and the second as cost. Whenever you see a capex headline, that is the question to ask.

Read-through

Where Microsoft's spending lands

A cloud company raising its spending budget is not an isolated event — that money becomes revenue for the companies it buys from. Microsoft guiding to $255–260B is a direct positive for the chip suppliers (NVDA, MRVL, INTC) and for the smaller cloud builders competing for the same demand (NBIS). This is most of why chips rose 8% today on news that was technically about a software company.

Nothing material

TSLA · GOOGL · PLTR · INTC · MRVL — no company-specific events in the past 24 hours.

But two of them moved hard

INTC rose about 12% and MRVL about 10% today. Neither announced anything. Both moves came from the sector — Lam Research rose 20% on strong results, Microsoft lifted its spending guide, and Samsung warned the memory shortage could run into 2028. This distinction matters: a sector move can reverse just as fast, because nothing changed at the company itself.
